# Editing, Style, and Voice

Apply [`../00-blog-writing-guardrails/SKILL.md`](../00-blog-writing-guardrails/SKILL.md) first; its gates apply to every post before this skill's guidance.

## 1. Plain-language self-edit gate
- Write for the reader's task, not the writer's expertise: lead with what the reader needs, use short sentences and everyday words, and test drafts for clarity before publishing. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]
- Define or cut jargon a general reader in the target audience would not already know; a plain-language rewrite must not remove technical precision the audience actually needs. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]

## 2. Cutting filler, hedging, and wordiness gate
- Interrogate every word in a sentence and remove or replace any word that does not contribute meaning; concise writing does not always have the fewest words, but it always uses the strongest ones. [PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]
- Cut hedging qualifiers ("somewhat," "fairly," "it seems," "in some cases") and redundant phrases that repeat an idea already stated, rather than leaving them in for a softer tone. [PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]
- Convert a phrase or clause into a single word or shorter phrase wherever the meaning survives intact, and watch for nominalizations (verbs turned into nouns, such as "make a decision" for "decide") that force extra helper verbs and lengthen the sentence without adding value. [PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]
- Combine sentences when one fact does not need a full sentence of its own and can be folded into an adjacent sentence without losing its value. [PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]

## 3. Active-voice preference gate
- Default to active voice, where the sentence's subject performs the action, for the majority of a post's sentences; active voice keeps meaning clear and sentences from becoming complicated or wordy. [PURDUEOWL-ACTIVEVOICE-01]
- Reserve passive voice for cases where the agent performing the action is genuinely obvious, unimportant, or unknown, or where the writer deliberately wants to foreground the action or the thing acted upon; do not default to passive voice as a stylistic habit. [PURDUEOWL-ACTIVEVOICE-01]

## 4. Consistent voice gate
- Keep person (first, second, or third), tense, and level of formality consistent within a post; do not drift from a direct second-person "you" into an impersonal third-person register mid-draft without a deliberate reason. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]
- Keep terminology consistent for the same concept throughout a post (pick one term and reuse it) rather than varying word choice for its own sake, since inconsistent terms read as inconsistent voice and cost the reader clarity. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]

## 5. Removing AI-tell phrasing gate
- Cut generic transitional filler that adds no reader value ("in conclusion," "it is important to note," "delve into," "in today's fast-paced world," "as we can see") in the same edit pass as other wordiness, since quality is judged on the finished content and this phrasing is a hallmark of automation that pads text without adding value. [GOOGLE-AICONTENT-01][PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]
- Replace vague, inflated phrasing with specific, concrete claims and numbers; if a sentence could appear unchanged in a post about any other topic, rewrite it with detail specific to this post's subject. [PURDUEOWL-CONCISE-01]

## 6. Pre-publish editing checklist
- Read the draft aloud (or have it read aloud) once for flow; a sentence that is hard to say is usually hard to read and needs a line edit. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]
- Confirm every paragraph passes the filler, hedging, active-voice, consistent-voice, and AI-tell checks above before marking the post ready, and confirm this pass does not contradict the guardrails' plain-language and pre-publish QA gates. [DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01][W3C-WCAG-01]
