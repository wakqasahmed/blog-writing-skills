# Persuasive Copywriting

Apply `../00-blog-writing-guardrails/SKILL.md` first; its originality, accuracy, and claims-substantiation gates apply to every persuasive claim below. A draft that fails a hard gate here must be marked `BLOCK`, not published as reader-ready.

## 1. Rhetorical-foundations gate (ethos, pathos, logos)
- Build persuasive copy on all three classical proofs, not just one: the writer's or organization's demonstrated character and credibility (ethos), the emotional state the copy puts the reader in (pathos), and the argument the copy itself makes (logos). [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01]
- Establish ethos before leaning on pathos: show the speaker is worthy of confidence (credentials, track record, transparent sourcing) rather than opening with emotional appeals that have no credibility behind them. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01]
- Do not substitute pathos for logos: an emotional hook can open a section, but the copy must still establish or appear to establish the true or apparently true case through its own argument, not through emotion alone. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01]

## 2. Influence-principles gate (Cialdini)
- When copy invokes reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, social proof, or unity to move a reader toward action, the underlying fact must be real and verifiable, not staged: real reviewers for social proof, a real credential for authority, a real limited quantity or deadline for scarcity. [CIALDINI-INFLUENCE-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]
- Do not fabricate or imply a false countdown, false limited stock, or false authority; scarcity and authority are principles that describe how real credible signals persuade, not license to manufacture fake ones. [CIALDINI-INFLUENCE-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]
- Use social proof (testimonials, adoption numbers, case studies) and authority (credentials, named experts) as reinforcement for the argument the copy already makes, not as a replacement for it. [CIALDINI-INFLUENCE-01]

## 3. Framing and loss-aversion gate
- Headlines, subheads, and CTA copy may frame the same underlying fact as an avoided loss or an achieved gain, but the framing must describe a real outcome the reader actually faces, not an invented cost. [KAHNEMAN-NOBEL-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]
- Because people weigh a loss more heavily than an equivalent gain, loss-framed copy ("stop losing X") is often more motivating than gain-framed copy ("start gaining X") describing the identical outcome — choose the frame deliberately rather than defaulting to gain-framing out of habit. [KAHNEMAN-NOBEL-01]
- Do not exploit loss aversion to manufacture urgency around a loss the reader was never actually going to incur; the reference point the copy implies must match the reader's real situation. [KAHNEMAN-NOBEL-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]

## 4. Objection-handling gate
- Name the reader's most likely objection to the offer or claim in the reader's own words before answering it; do not let an unaddressed objection sit as the reader's last thought before the CTA. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01]
- Answer each named objection with a verifiable fact, source, or guarantee, not a restated assertion of confidence; an objection answered with more enthusiasm instead of more evidence still leaves the objection standing. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]
- Where the objection concerns a factual claim, resolve it with the same substantiation the accuracy gate already requires, rather than treating persuasive copy as exempt from that gate. [FTC-ADSUB-01]

## 5. CTA-design gate
- State the single action the reader should take next in plain, specific language ("Start your free trial", not "Learn more") so the CTA itself carries logos — the reader can see exactly what happens if they click. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01][DIGITALGOV-PLAIN-01]
- Attach the CTA to the influence principle and frame that actually apply to the offer (a real deadline, a real credential, a real avoided cost) rather than a generic urgency phrase with nothing real behind it. [CIALDINI-INFLUENCE-01][KAHNEMAN-NOBEL-01]
- Give every CTA that is an image or icon equivalent alternative text describing the action, so the persuasive intent survives for assistive-technology readers. [W3C-WCAG-01]

## 6. Pre-publish persuasion QA gate
- Re-read the draft's headline, intro, and CTA together and confirm each invoked influence principle, frame, and emotional appeal points back to a fact the writer can substantiate on request. [CIALDINI-INFLUENCE-01][FTC-ADSUB-01]
- Confirm at least one real objection has been named and answered with evidence before the final CTA, and that the CTA states one specific next action. [ARISTOTLE-RHETORIC-01]
